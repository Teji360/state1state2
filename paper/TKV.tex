\begin{abstract}
The key-value (KV) cache is the dominant memory bottleneck in long-context
autoregressive inference. Existing online compression methods face a
tradeoff: Oja's rule (used by OjaKV) is cheap but gives no per-token
optimality guarantee and drifts at long context, while Brand's exact
rank-1 SVD update is provably optimal but costs $\mathcal{O}(R^3 + TR^2)$
per token --- growing with context length. We propose \textbf{TKV} (Tensor
KV), which resolves this by running Brand's update only through a bounded
warmup window, then freezing the resulting basis and projecting subsequent
tokens onto it at $\mathcal{O}(R{\cdot}d)$ per token indefinitely. A
$\Phi$-bridge reuses the Gaussian matrix already used for token-importance
tracking as the Halko randomized range finder for cold-start
initialization, and the update is implemented with XLA-static shapes for
GPU/TPU compatibility. On real Mistral-7B activations, a TKV rank pyramid
(avg.\ rank 15) reaches perplexity 20.1 versus an exact-cache baseline of
19.4 (3.6\% degradation), while the same pyramid schedule applied to
OjaKV degrades to 60$\times$ worse than exact; at 128K-token context TKV
achieves 8.5$\times$ memory compression and lower reconstruction error
than OjaKV at every long-context length tested (1K--32K tokens).
Importance-weighted cold start further reduces important-token
reconstruction error by 90--95\% at rank 8--16.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The key-value (KV) cache is the dominant memory bottleneck in autoregressive
transformer inference: for $L$ layers, $H$ KV heads, head dimension $d$,
and context length $T$, the dense cache requires $\mathcal{O}(2LHdT)$
floats, becoming the binding constraint on batch size, GPU utilization,
and serving cost as context scales to 128K+ tokens.

The predominant approach is low-rank compression: represent the key matrix
$K \in \R^{T \times d}$ as a rank-$R$ factorization $U \Sigma V^\top$ with
$R \ll \min(T, d)$, reducing memory from $\mathcal{O}(Td)$ to
$\mathcal{O}(R(T+d))$.  The key challenge is \emph{when} to compute this
factorization.  Offline methods (SVDq \citep{svdq2025}, xKV \citep{xkv2025},
KVTC \citep{kvtc2026}) run a batch SVD after the full sequence is
available, incompatible with streaming generation; online methods must
update the factorization incrementally after each new token.  OjaKV
\citep{ojakov2025}, the current online state of the art, applies Oja's
rule (stochastic gradient PCA) token-by-token --- asymptotically
convergent but with no per-step optimality guarantee.  When OjaKV's
authors considered Brand's exact rank-1 update as an alternative, they
rejected it as ``too slow'' without a systematic evaluation.

\textbf{This work.}  We revisit that judgment for a bounded warmup window,
then \emph{freeze} the resulting basis for the remainder of the sequence
rather than running Brand's exact update ($\mathcal{O}(R^3+TR^2)$ per
token) forever, which would otherwise let its cost compound at long
context (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).  During warmup itself, exact
updates are cheap and substantially more accurate than Oja's rule
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}); the freeze transition preserves this
advantage while holding per-token cost at $\mathcal{O}(R{\cdot}d)$
indefinitely.  We also identify a structural insight missed by prior
work: the random Gaussian matrix used for token-importance scoring
satisfies exactly the distributional requirements of Halko's randomized
SVD range finder, so reusing it (\emph{the $\Phi$-bridge}) unifies two
previously separate operations and eliminates a distinct SVD warm-up cost.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The $\Phi$-bridge: a theoretical and practical connection between
        random linear sketching and Halko's randomized SVD (Section~\ref{sec:phi}).
  \item To our knowledge, the first online KV compression method with a
        per-token EYM optimality guarantee through a warmup window, followed
        by a freeze transition that holds per-token cost at
        $\mathcal{O}(R{\cdot}d)$ indefinitely
        (Sections~\ref{sec:brand}--\ref{sec:freeze}).
  \item An XLA-compilable implementation of Brand's dynamic rank-1 update using
        static tensor shapes (Section~\ref{sec:xla}).
  \item Importance-weighted cold start that reduces important-token reconstruction
        error by 90--95\% at rank 8--16 (Section~\ref{sec:importance}).
  \item Empirical evaluation against OjaKV under identical constraints, showing
        consistent quality improvement at the cost of moderate per-token overhead
        (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{KV cache structure and EYM.}
In a transformer with head dimension $d$, after processing $t$ tokens the KV
cache for one head is a matrix $K \in \R^{t \times d}$ (and similarly $V$);
each new token appends one row, and compression approximates
$K \approx \hat{K} = U_K \Sigma_K V_K^\top$ with $R \leq \min(t, d)$.  By the
Eckart-Young-Mirsky (EYM) theorem, the best rank-$R$ approximation of any
matrix $A$ in Frobenius or spectral norm is its truncated SVD
$\hat{A}_R = U_R \Sigma_R V_R^\top$, so any online method that maintains a
valid truncated SVD after each update is EYM-optimal for that update.

\paragraph{Brand's rank-1 update \citep{brand2006} and Halko randomized SVD \citep{halko2011}.}
Given a factorization $A = U \Sigma V^\top$ and a new row $\mathbf{x}$,
Brand's update computes the exact SVD of $\tilde{A} = [A; \mathbf{x}]$ in
closed form by factoring a small $(R+1) \times (R+1)$ augmented matrix and
truncating back to rank $R$ (an exact EYM step per token), then updating
$U$ via an $\mathcal{O}(TR^2)$ rotation (Section~\ref{sec:brand} gives the
full algorithm).  Halko's randomized SVD instead sketches $A \in
\R^{m \times n}$ with a random $\Omega \in \R^{n \times R}$: the range of
$A\Omega$ approximates $A$'s column space, and QR on $A\Omega$ followed by
the compact SVD of $Q^\top A$ gives the randomized SVD at
$\mathcal{O}(mnR)$ cost vs.\ $\mathcal{O}(mn^2)$ for exact SVD, with
high-probability error bounds \citep{halko2011}.

\paragraph{Oja's rule \citep{oja1982}.}
For streaming PCA, Oja's rule updates the $R \times d$ basis $V$ after each
observation $\mathbf{x}$ as $V \leftarrow V + \eta_t (V\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}^\top$,
$V \leftarrow \mathrm{QR}(V^\top)^\top$, with step size $\eta_t = 1/(t+1)$
--- the update used by OjaKV.  Unlike Brand's update, this is a stochastic
gradient step: each intermediate $V$ is an approximation, not an exact
truncated SVD.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\Phi$}{Phi}-Bridge}
\label{sec:phi}

A random Gaussian sketch tracks token importance via a random projection
matrix $\Phi \in \R^{R \times d}$: for a token vector $\mathbf{x} \in \R^d$,
$\Phi \mathbf{x} \in \R^R$ captures token statistics in compressed form.
Halko's randomized range finder requires exactly the same object --- a
random matrix $\Omega \in \R^{d \times R}$ whose product $K\Omega$ forms a
randomized range basis for the row space of $K$.

\textbf{Observation.}  Setting $\Omega = \Phi^\top$ reuses the same
Gaussian matrix for both roles: $K\Phi^\top \in \R^{T \times R}$
approximates the principal right singular vectors of $K$ while $\Phi$
simultaneously encodes token importance statistics.  This unification ---
the \emph{$\Phi$-bridge} --- works because the Gaussian distribution of
$\Phi$ satisfies the requirements for both linear importance sketching
(Johnson-Lindenstrauss concentration) and Halko's range finder
(sub-Gaussian rows, Theorem~1.1 of \citep{halko2011}); this differs from
the structured hashing of the classical count-min sketch
\citep{cormode2005}, which shares only the general idea of random linear
projection for dimensionality reduction.

\subsection{Cold Start via Halko Initialization}
\label{sec:coldstart}

After observing a prefix of $T_0$ tokens, TKV initializes the SVD factorization
via the randomized range finder:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Halko Cold Start with $\Phi$-Bridge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $K \in \R^{T_0 \times d}$, $V \in \R^{T_0 \times d}$, $\Phi \in \R^{R \times d}$
\State $Y \leftarrow K \Phi^\top$  \hfill \textit{randomized sketch, $\mathcal{O}(T_0 d R)$}
\State $Q, \_ \leftarrow \mathrm{QR}(Y)$  \hfill \textit{approximate range basis}
\State $B \leftarrow Q^\top K$  \hfill \textit{project into low-dimensional space}
\State $\hat{U}, \hat{\Sigma}, \hat{V}^\top \leftarrow \mathrm{SVD}(B)$  \hfill \textit{small SVD, $\mathcal{O}(R^2 d)$}
\State $U_K \leftarrow Q \hat{U}$, $\Sigma_K \leftarrow \hat{\Sigma}$, $V_K \leftarrow \hat{V}$
\State \Return $(U_K, \Sigma_K, V_K)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

If importance weights $w_i \geq 0$ are available (e.g., from prior attention
statistics), we apply them as row weights: $Y \leftarrow \mathrm{diag}(w)K\Phi^\top$,
biasing the range finder toward the subspace of high-importance tokens.
This cold start opens TKV's \emph{warmup phase}: it is followed by exact
Brand updates (Section~\ref{sec:brand}) for the first $T_0$ (``warmup\_len'')
tokens, after which the basis is frozen (Section~\ref{sec:freeze}).

\subsection{Streaming Brand Updates (Warmup Phase)}
\label{sec:brand}

During warmup, each new token $(\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{v})$ triggers one Brand
rank-1 update:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Brand Rank-1 Update (per token)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $(U_K, \Sigma_K, V_K)$, new key $\mathbf{k} \in \R^d$, rank budget $R$
\State $\mathbf{c} \leftarrow V_K \mathbf{k}$  \hfill \textit{project onto current row space}
\State $\mathbf{p} \leftarrow \mathbf{k} - V_K^\top \mathbf{c}$,
       $\hat{\mathbf{p}} \leftarrow \mathbf{p}/\|\mathbf{p}\|$
\State Form $K_{\mathrm{aug}} \in \R^{(R+1)\times(R+1)}$ with diagonal $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, row extension $\mathbf{c}$, and corner $\|\mathbf{p}\|$
\State $\hat{U}, \hat{\Sigma}, \hat{W}^\top \leftarrow \mathrm{SVD}(K_{\mathrm{aug}})$
\State $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \leftarrow \hat{\Sigma}_{:R}$  \hfill \textit{truncate to rank $R$ (EYM step)}
\State Extend $V_K$ with $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$, rotate: $V_K \leftarrow \hat{W}_{:R} [V_K; \hat{\mathbf{p}}^\top]$
\State Append new $U_K$ row, rotate: $U_K \leftarrow [U_K; \mathbf{0}^\top] \hat{U}_{:R,:R}$
\State \Return $(U_K, \boldsymbol{\sigma}, V_K)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The truncation at step~5 is exactly an EYM projection: the resulting
$\hat{U}_{:R} \hat{\Sigma}_{:R} \hat{V}_{:R}^\top$ is the best rank-$R$
approximation of $[K; \mathbf{k}^\top]$ in Frobenius norm.  This guarantee
holds after every token \emph{during the warmup phase}, i.e., for
$t \leq T_0$; Section~\ref{sec:freeze} describes the post-warmup regime.

\subsection{Freeze Transition (Decode Phase)}
\label{sec:freeze}

Once $t = T_0$, TKV freezes the basis $(V_K, V_V)$ produced by the most
recent Brand update and stops running Algorithm~2.  Every subsequent token
is instead projected onto this fixed basis:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{TKV Frozen-Phase Token Update (per token, $t > T_0$)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Frozen basis $V_K \in \R^{R \times d}$, stored projections
         $\mathrm{proj}_K \in \R^{(t-1) \times R}$, new key $\mathbf{k} \in \R^d$
\State $\mathbf{c} \leftarrow V_K \mathbf{k}$ \hfill \textit{project onto the frozen basis, $\mathcal{O}(Rd)$}
\State Append $\mathbf{c}$ to $\mathrm{proj}_K$ \hfill \textit{no SVD, no rotation}
\State \Return $\mathrm{proj}_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This is implemented by \texttt{tkv\_add\_token} in our reference
implementation.  The motivation is cost: Brand's update is
$\mathcal{O}(R^3 + TR^2)$ per token, so running it indefinitely makes
per-token cost grow with context length.  Freezing after a bounded warmup
caps per-token cost at $\mathcal{O}(Rd)$ for the rest of the sequence,
regardless of how long it runs --- the gap between the two reaches
$13.4\times$ by $T=16{,}000$ (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

The honest tradeoff (stated precisely in Section~\ref{sec:theory}) is that
frozen-phase tokens are no longer individually EYM-optimal, since $V_K$
was fit to $K_{T_0}$, not the full sequence.  Empirically this is
favorable regardless: OjaKV's basis keeps rotating and its projection
error compounds with $t$, while TKV's frozen projection error does not, so
TKV's fixed-but-accurate basis still outperforms OjaKV's
continuously-drifting one at every long-context length we tested
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\subsection{XLA-Static Shape Implementation}
\label{sec:xla}

Brand's update is inherently dynamic ($U_K$ grows by one row per token), but
static-shape XLA compilation requires a fixed pre-allocated buffer
$\hat{U} \in \R^{T_{\max} \times R}$ with the logical sequence length
tracked separately; new rows are written via
\texttt{jax.lax.dynamic\_update\_slice}, which supports dynamic-index
writes while keeping a static shape graph, and the $\mathcal{O}(TR^2)$
U-rotation operates on this buffer bounded by $T_{\max}$.  To our
knowledge, no prior work has implemented Brand updates in XLA-compilable
form, a prerequisite for GPU/TPU deployment.

\subsection{Importance-Weighted Cold Start}
\label{sec:importance}

During autoregressive generation, attention weight statistics accumulate per
token position.  Following Moment-KV \citep{momentkv2024}, we maintain an
EMA importance score $I_i \leftarrow \alpha I_i + (1-\alpha) \cdot \bar{w}_i$,
where $\bar{w}_i = \frac{1}{n_q} \sum_{q} \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{q} K^\top / \sqrt{d})_{q,i}$
is the mean attention weight token $i$ receives across queries.
When the context must be re-compressed (e.g., at context-length boundaries),
these scores serve as row weights in the Halko cold start, biasing the initial
basis toward tokens that are actually attended to.  Section~\ref{sec:experiments}
shows this reduces important-token reconstruction error by 90--95\% at rank 8--16.

\subsection{Compressed Attention}
\label{sec:attn}

Attention can be computed directly from the SVD factors without explicit
reconstruction of $K$ or $V$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{s} &= \mathbf{q} K^\top = \mathbf{q} V_K^\top \Sigma_K U_K^\top
               \in \R^T, \qquad
  \mathbf{out} = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{s}) U_V \Sigma_V V_V \in \R^d,
\end{align}
requiring only matrix-vector products with the rank-$R$ factors
($\mathcal{O}(R(T{+}d))$ vs.\ $\mathcal{O}(Td)$ for dense attention).  In the
frozen phase, the same formula applies with $U_K \Sigma_K$ replaced by the
stored projections $\mathrm{proj}_K$, so compressed attention is computed
identically before and after the freeze.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

The following results follow directly from applying Brand's exact rank-1
update iteratively under the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem; the contribution
is identifying that this combination maintains EYM-optimality token-by-token
in the KV cache setting, a property that does not hold for Oja's rule.

\begin{corollary}[Per-Token EYM Optimality, Warmup Phase]
\label{cor:eym}
Let $K_t \in \R^{t \times d}$ be the key matrix after $t \leq T_0$ tokens
(i.e., during the warmup window), and let
$(U_t, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t, V_t)$ be the TKV state after processing the $t$-th
token via Algorithm~2.  Then
$\| K_t - U_t \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_t) V_t^\top \|_F
  = \min_{\mathrm{rank}(B) \leq R} \| K_t - B \|_F$
(the Brand update at step $t$ truncates the exact SVD of
$K_{\mathrm{aug}} = [K_{t-1}; \mathbf{k}_t^\top]$ to rank $R$, which by EYM
is the best such approximation).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Post-Freeze Cost and Quality]
\label{prop:postfreeze}
For $t > T_0$, TKV's basis $(V_{T_0,K}, V_{T_0,V})$ is held fixed and each
new token is processed in $\mathcal{O}(Rd)$ time via projection onto it
(Algorithm~3), with \textbf{no further EYM updates}: the projection is exact
within the rowspace of $V_{T_0,K}$ but not in general the EYM-optimal
rank-$R$ approximation of $K_t$, since $V_{T_0,K}$ was optimized only for
$K_{T_0}$.  Quality after freezing is therefore bounded by how well the
warmup-window subspace continues to span later tokens' directions, not by a
fresh per-token optimality guarantee.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Comparison to OjaKV]
For $t \leq T_0$, TKV achieves the minimum Frobenius reconstruction error
among all rank-$R$ factorizations of $K_t$, while OjaKV's stochastic-gradient
state has no analogous guarantee and can exceed the EYM optimum at any
finite $t$.  For $t > T_0$ neither method retains a strict guarantee
(Proposition~\ref{prop:postfreeze}), but empirically TKV's
fixed-but-accurate basis still outperforms OjaKV's continuously-drifting
one at every long-context length tested, since OjaKV's staleness error
compounds with $t$ while TKV's frozen-basis error does not
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Cold Start Error Bound]
After the Halko cold start with oversampling parameter $p$, the TKV
approximation satisfies, with probability at least $1 - \delta$,
$\| K - \hat{K} \|_F \leq
  \left(1 + \sqrt{R/(p+1)} \cdot t_\delta \right) \sigma_{R+1}(K)$,
where $\sigma_{R+1}$ is the $(R+1)$-th singular value of $K$ and $t_\delta$ is
a tail factor from the Halko error bound (Theorem~1.1 in \citep{halko2011}).
Brand updates maintain this quality exactly through $t = T_0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:postfreeze} covers the regime beyond).
\end{proposition}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Real-model experiments (reconstruction, BrandOnly-vs-OjaKV head-to-head,
perplexity, decode latency) use \emph{real} K/V activations captured from
Mistral-7B-v0.1 on WikiText-2 via a lightweight passthrough hook, with no
compression applied during extraction; synthetic-data experiments (memory
formula, long-context reconstruction, importance weighting) use $d=64$
throughout, independent of the deployment model.  All experiments use
JAX~0.4 and $T_{\max}=1024$ where applicable; timings are Python/eager
mode on CPU (GPU numbers would differ due to XLA compilation benefits).

\subsection{TKV Long-Context Reconstruction vs OjaKV}
\label{sec:longctx}

The ablation above shows BrandOnly's warmup mechanism is accurate, but
Brand's update cannot run forever at long context: the $13.4\times$
update-time gap at $T=16{,}000$ (Section~\ref{sec:freeze}) is exactly why
TKV freezes after a bounded warmup.  We now evaluate TKV itself --- warmup
through $T_0=512$ tokens via Brand updates, then frozen --- against OjaKV
(which streams throughout) at long context.  K/V matrices here are
synthetic, with singular values decaying as $1/\sqrt{i+1}$ across the
$d=64$ feature dimensions, allowing controlled sweeps to $T=32{,}000$ tokens
without the cost of extracting that much real Mistral-7B context.

TKV wins at every $(T, R)$ tested, with the advantage growing at higher
rank (full numbers in Appendix~\ref{app:longctx}); Figure~\ref{fig:longctx}
shows the trend visually.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{benchmark_tkv_reconstruction.pdf}
\caption{Left: TKV vs OjaKV reconstruction error vs sequence length, by rank.
Right: TKV error vs rank, by sequence length.  TKV's error stays essentially
flat as $T$ grows past the $T_0=512$ freeze point, while OjaKV's drifting
basis degrades.}
\label{fig:longctx}
\end{figure}

TKV's error is nearly constant past the freeze point (e.g., at $R=16$:
$0.537 \to 0.548 \to 0.549$ from $T=1$K to $32$K) despite receiving no
further SVD updates, while OjaKV's error continues to be dominated by basis
staleness.

\paragraph{Decode-phase latency.}  Using real Mistral-7B KV activations with
a shared 256-token warmup, TKV's frozen-phase update costs $0.19$\,ms per
decode token at ranks $8$--$32$ --- $2.6$--$3.0\times$ faster than OjaKV
and roughly $10$--$11\times$ faster than continuing to run Brand's update
(BrandOnly), which is exactly the cost TKV's freeze avoids.  At rank~64
this advantage reverses (TKV $0.77$\,ms vs.\ OjaKV's $0.57$\,ms); we lack a
confident mechanistic explanation and flag it as an open observation
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) rather than a settled result (full
per-rank numbers in Appendix~\ref{app:decode}).

\subsection{Memory Compression}

At $T=512$, $d=64$, the memory ratio (compressed/dense) is
$0.070/0.141/0.282/0.563$ for $R=4/8/16/32$ --- at rank~16, this corresponds to 28.2\% of the dense cache memory, where the
BrandOnly ablation (Section~\ref{sec:brandonly-ablation}) shows a
1.95$\times$ reconstruction advantage over OjaKV at the same rank ($T=512$,
Table~\ref{tab:recon}).  At rank~32 (56.3\% of dense memory), the Halko
cold-start ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ppl}) is effectively lossless on
Mistral-7B too (ratio 0.999, Appendix~\ref{app:ppl}).  At the scale TKV
targets, the same storage format gives a much larger absolute saving: at
$T=128$K tokens, a TKV rank pyramid (average rank~15) gives a 4.3$\times$
compression at GPT-2 scale that holds consistently from $T=1$K to $128$K
(full breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:memory}).  On Mistral-7B's real
architecture (32 layers, 8 KV heads, $d=128$), the same pyramid gives an
even larger 8.5$\times$ compression at the same scale (3.94\,GB versus
33.55\,GB at 128K tokens, Table~\ref{tab:longmem-mistral}) --- grouped-query
attention caches fewer KV heads per layer than GPT-2's multi-head attention,
so TKV's per-KV-head compression compounds into a larger overall saving on
the architecture modern long-context models actually use.

\subsection{Perplexity (Mistral-7B)}
\label{sec:ppl}

We evaluate two configurations on Mistral-7B (32 layers, 8 KV heads via
grouped-query attention, $d=128$), measuring perplexity on a small held-out
set of technical sentences.  We note that this is a proof-of-concept
evaluation; results on WikiText-2 or PG-19 are needed to make strong claims
about end-to-end perplexity (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Halko cold-start, uniform rank (ablation).}  We first measure a
single Halko cold-start SVD per forward call with no Brand streaming and no
freeze, at a single uniform rank applied to every layer --- this isolates
the cold-start mechanism's lossiness on its own, since streaming Brand
correctness is already validated on real activations by
Section~\ref{sec:brandonly-ablation}.

Exact perplexity is 19.39; full per-rank numbers are in
Appendix~\ref{app:ppl} and Figure~\ref{fig:perplexity}.
At ranks 8 and 16, perplexity is far above exact (14.2$\times$ and
4.8$\times$ worse); the knee falls between rank~16 and rank~32, reaching
lossless perplexity at 50.2\% of dense memory ($T=128$).  This knee is
sharper and shifted higher than GPT-2's (lossless by rank~32 of $d=64$),
since Mistral's larger head dimension means a fixed absolute rank covers a
smaller subspace fraction.  At rank~64 the memory ratio exceeds 1.0,
underscoring that compression only pays off when $R \ll \min(T, d)$.

\paragraph{TKV, per-layer rank pyramid.}  We next apply TKV's freeze
mechanism with PyramidKV-style layer ranks (higher rank for early layers,
lower for later ones, average rank~15) and compare against the same pyramid
schedule applied to OjaKV.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Offline KV compression.}
SVDq \citep{svdq2025}, xKV \citep{xkv2025}, KVTC \citep{kvtc2026}, and
KQ-SVD \citep{kqsvd2025} all apply batch or cross-layer SVD with offline
calibration (per-prompt, cross-layer, or attention-matrix decomposition
respectively), achieving strong compression but incompatible with streaming
generation.

\paragraph{Online KV compression.}
OjaKV \citep{ojakov2025} is the closest prior work: Oja's rule applied
token-by-token to maintain an approximate, calibration-free PCA basis with
no per-token optimality guarantee.  LoRC \citep{lorc2024} uses truncated
SVD per layer offline.  StreamingLLM \citep{streamingllm2023} keeps only
sink tokens and a recent window in full precision, orthogonal to low-rank
compression; TKV composes directly with this idea (\emph{TKV+Sink}, full
evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Randomized linear algebra.}
Halko's framework \citep{halko2011} is standard for large-scale randomized
SVD, and Brand's update \citep{brand2006} is well-known in numerical linear
algebra but, to our knowledge, not previously applied to KV cache
compression.  The $\Phi$-bridge's use of a dense Gaussian matrix
satisfying both Johnson-Lindenstrauss and Halko's sub-Gaussian row
requirement (vs.\ the structured hashing of the count-min sketch
\citep{cormode2005}) is a connection novel to this work.

%% ============================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Scope of evaluation.}  Results cover GPT-2 small and Mistral-7B; a
single (layer, KV-head) pair for the headline ablation, broadened to a
12-configuration sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:multihead}) and a pyramid
schedule across all layers/heads for perplexity and long-context.  Llama,
Qwen, real (non-synthetic) long-context activations, and a systematic
pyramid-schedule sweep across scales remain open.

\textbf{Evaluation gaps.}  Importance weighting uses oracle rather than
real EMA attention weights; long-context benchmarks use synthetic,
non-shifting statistics, so a pathological distribution shift after the
warmup window $T_0$ is not yet evaluated empirically
(Proposition~\ref{prop:postfreeze} bounds this risk theoretically).
Perplexity uses a small held-out set rather than full WikiText-2, and
\emph{TKV+Sink} and dynamic rank scheduling are implemented but not yet
benchmarked end-to-end.

\textbf{Performance.}  BrandOnly is 3.7--4.1$\times$ slower than OjaKV
during warmup; once frozen, TKV is 2.6--3.0$\times$ faster at ranks 8--32
but reverses at rank~64 for reasons we do not yet understand.  A GPU
evaluation is required before deployment.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented TKV, an online KV cache compression system combining the
$\Phi$-bridge (Gaussian sketch reused as Halko range-finder), Halko cold
start and Brand's exact rank-1 update for a bounded warmup window, and a
freeze transition that holds per-token cost at $\mathcal{O}(R{\cdot}d)$
indefinitely thereafter --- to our knowledge, the first online KV
compression method with a per-token EYM optimality guarantee through
warmup, followed by a freeze transition that avoids Brand's update cost
from compounding at long context.  On Mistral-7B's real architecture, a TKV
rank pyramid reaches 8.5$\times$ memory compression at 128K tokens while
landing within 3.6\% of exact-cache perplexity, versus OjaKV's $60\times$
degradation under the identical rank budget --- a margin that holds (and on
GPT-2 small, reverses in TKV's favor) at both model scales we tested.

The core insight is that exact rank-1 updates (Brand) and randomized warm-up
(Halko) are compatible during a bounded window, and that freezing the
resulting basis afterward --- rather than continuing to update it exactly
forever, or letting it drift as in Oja's rule --- is what makes the
combination viable at long context.  The $\Phi$-bridge unifies this
pipeline with the importance-tracking data structure at no additional cost.
